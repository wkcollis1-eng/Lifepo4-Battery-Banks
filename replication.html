🔧 Replication Guide

Step-by-step instructions for setting up your own LiFePO₄ battery monitoring study

This guide walks through the complete process of replicating the 94-day battery monitoring study, from hardware selection through data collection and analysis. Whether you're validating architectural immunity or simply implementing robust battery monitoring, these steps provide a proven methodology.

Time commitment: 2-4 hours for hardware setup, plus ongoing data collection (minimum 30 days recommended).

Skill level: Intermediate electrical/electronics experience recommended. Familiarity with basic programming helpful but not required.

Lithium batteries can deliver thousands of amps in a short circuit and store enough energy to cause serious injury or fire. If you are not comfortable working with high-current DC systems and battery technology, seek assistance from someone with appropriate experience. This guide assumes you understand basic electrical safety and battery handling procedures.

Part 1: Equipment Selection

Required Components

Battery Bank

  • Chemistry: LiFePO₄ (LFP)
  • Capacity: ≥100 Ah recommended
  • Configuration: 12V (4S) or 24V (8S)
  • Quality: Grade A cells

Battery Management System

  • Type: Appropriate for cell count (4S, 8S, etc.)
  • Current rating: ≥100A recommended
  • Features: Over/under voltage, temp protection
  • Example: JBD/Xiaoxiang 4S 100-200A

Voltage Monitor

  • Resolution: ≤10 mV
  • Accuracy: ±1% or better
  • Interface: Bluetooth, WiFi, or serial
  • Example: Victron SmartShunt 500A

Data Logger

  • Platform: Home Assistant, Raspberry Pi, Arduino, etc.
  • Storage: Time-series database or CSV
  • Sampling: 10-60 second intervals
  • Uptime: >99% reliability

Safety Equipment

  • Fuses: Class T DC-rated fuses
  • Fuse holders: Appropriate for current
  • Fire extinguisher: Class D (metal fires)
  • Smoke detector: Near battery location

Optional Enhancements

  • Temperature sensors (ambient + battery)
  • Fireproof battery enclosure
  • DC circuit breaker or disconnect
  • Multimeter for calibration verification

Part 2: Physical Assembly

Step 1
Cell Configuration & Bus Bars

For parallel cells:

  1. Ensure all cells are at similar voltage (within 100 mV) before first connection
  2. Use thick copper bus bars (appropriate for current rating):
    • 100A system: ≥6 AWG / 10mm²
    • 200A system: ≥4 AWG / 16mm²
    • 500A system: ≥2 AWG / 25mm²
  3. Keep all connection resistances as equal as possible
  4. Use star topology for parallel connections (not daisy chain)
  5. Torque all terminals to manufacturer specifications
Star topology: All positive terminals connect to a central positive bus bar, all negative terminals to a central negative bus bar. This ensures equal resistance paths and prevents one cell from carrying disproportionate current.
Step 2
BMS Installation
  1. Connect BMS balance wires to each cell in the series string:
    • B- to battery negative
    • B0 to cell 1 positive (= cell 2 negative)
    • B1 to cell 2 positive (= cell 3 negative)
    • Continue for all cells in series
  2. Verify polarity BEFORE connecting power wires
  3. Route balance wires away from high-current paths
  4. Secure BMS in location with adequate ventilation
Double-check all balance wire connections. Reversed polarity can destroy the BMS and potentially cause battery damage or fire.
Step 3
Fuse Installation
  1. Select Class T fuses rated for:
    • Voltage: ≥1.5× system voltage (e.g., ≥32V for 12V system)
    • Current: Based on maximum expected load + 25% safety margin
  2. Install fuses as close to battery positive terminal as possible (within 7 inches / 18 cm)
  3. Use properly rated fuse holders with low contact resistance
  4. Consider installing fuses on both positive AND negative sides for maximum safety
Fuse sizing example: For a 500Ah 12V bank with expected 50A continuous load:
• Minimum: 50A × 1.25 = 63A fuse
• Recommended: 80A or 100A Class T fuse (allows for surge currents)
Step 4
SmartShunt Installation
  1. Install SmartShunt on the negative side of the battery bank
  2. All negative loads must flow through the shunt for accurate current measurement
  3. Wiring order (from battery to loads):
    • Battery negative → SmartShunt battery terminal
    • SmartShunt load terminal → All loads/chargers negative
  4. Connect voltage sense wire (red) to battery positive (or BMS positive output)
  5. Ensure all connections are tight and clean (low resistance)
The SmartShunt measures voltage at its sense wire connection point. For bus-level monitoring (as in this study), connect sense wire directly to the main positive bus bar, not to individual cell terminals.
Step 5
Temperature Monitoring (Optional)
  1. Place ambient temperature sensor near battery location (not in direct sunlight)
  2. For battery core temperature, attach sensor to middle cells:
    • Use thermal paste or adhesive pad for good contact
    • Insulate sensor from air currents
    • Route wires away from terminals
  3. Connect to data logger via appropriate interface (1-Wire, I2C, analog, etc.)

Safety Verification Checklist

  • All terminals torqued to specification
  • No exposed conductors or loose wires
  • Fuses installed and properly rated
  • BMS balance wires connected with correct polarity
  • Fire extinguisher accessible
  • Smoke detector installed and tested
  • Area well-ventilated
  • Flammable materials removed from battery area

Part 3: Sensor Calibration & Configuration

Step 6
SmartShunt Configuration
  1. Download VictronConnect app (iOS, Android, or Windows)
  2. Connect to SmartShunt via Bluetooth
  3. Configure battery parameters:
    • Battery capacity: Enter rated Ah (e.g., 500 Ah)
    • Charged voltage: 13.6V for 12V LiFePO₄
    • Tail current: 4% of capacity (e.g., 20A for 500Ah)
    • Charged detection time: 3 minutes
    • Peukert exponent: 1.05 for LiFePO₄
  4. Zero current measurement:
    • Disconnect all loads and chargers
    • Wait 1 minute for stabilization
    • Select "Zero current" in app settings
  5. Verify voltage reading matches a quality multimeter (±10 mV)
Step 7
BMS Configuration
  1. Connect to BMS via Bluetooth using manufacturer app (e.g., Xiaoxiang BMS app)
  2. Configure protection parameters:
    Parameter Recommended Value (12V LiFePO₄)
    Over-voltage protection (OVP) 3.65V per cell (14.6V total)
    Over-voltage release 3.55V per cell (14.2V total)
    Under-voltage protection (UVP) 2.7V per cell (10.8V total)
    Under-voltage release 3.0V per cell (12.0V total)
    Charge over-current 0.5C (e.g., 250A for 500Ah)
    Discharge over-current 1C (e.g., 500A for 500Ah)
    Over-temperature (charge) 45°C (113°F)
    Under-temperature (charge) 0°C (32°F)
  3. Enable balance function if available (typical threshold: 30-50 mV difference)
  4. Record baseline cell voltages for reference

Part 4: Data Logging Setup

Step 8
Home Assistant Setup (Recommended)

Option A: Using Home Assistant

  1. Install Home Assistant on Raspberry Pi or dedicated hardware
  2. Add Victron integration:
    • Configuration → Integrations → Add Integration
    • Search for "Victron" and follow setup wizard
    • SmartShunt should auto-discover via Bluetooth
  3. Configure InfluxDB for time-series storage:
    • Install InfluxDB add-on
    • Enable in configuration.yaml
    • Select battery entities to record
  4. Create automations for data export:
    • Daily CSV export as backup
    • Alert on data gaps or sensor failures

Option B: Direct Python Logging

  1. Install Python libraries: bleak (Bluetooth), pandas, sqlite3
  2. Write script to poll SmartShunt via Bluetooth every 10-60 seconds
  3. Store readings in SQLite database or CSV file
  4. Run as systemd service for automatic startup
Sample Python skeleton available in repository: scripts/smartshunt_logger.py
Step 9
Data Collection Verification
  1. Run data logger for 24 hours as a test
  2. Verify data is being recorded correctly:
    • Check for gaps in timestamps (should be <1% missing)
    • Verify voltage values are reasonable (12-14V range for 12V LiFePO₄)
    • Confirm sampling interval is consistent
  3. Set up automated backup:
    • Daily export to external storage (USB, network drive, cloud)
    • Multiple backup locations recommended
  4. Test data logger restart/recovery:
    • Reboot system, verify logging resumes automatically
    • Disconnect power, verify graceful recovery

Part 5: Study Execution

Step 10
Initial Capacity Test (Optional but Recommended)
  1. Fully charge battery to 14.6V (BMS limit)
  2. Let battery rest for 2+ hours
  3. Discharge at constant current (0.2C recommended):
    • 100Ah bank: 20A discharge
    • 500Ah bank: 100A discharge
  4. Continue until BMS disconnects (typically 10.8V)
  5. Record total Ah delivered (SmartShunt tracks this)
  6. Compare to rated capacity
Capacity test validates your bank's baseline performance before long-term monitoring begins. Expected: ≥95% of rated capacity for Grade A cells.
Step 11
Begin Monitoring Period
  1. After capacity test (or from current state), let battery rest without load
  2. Verify data logging is running continuously
  3. Record study start date and initial conditions:
    • Starting voltage
    • Ambient temperature
    • Cell individual voltages (from BMS)
    • Any recent charge/discharge activity
  4. Minimize battery disturbances:
    • No charging (unless testing specific scenarios)
    • No loads (pure stasis monitoring)
    • Stable temperature environment
  5. Check data collection weekly:
    • Verify no gaps or sensor failures
    • Confirm backups are working
    • Spot-check voltage readings for reasonableness
Minimum study duration: 30 days provides meaningful drift data. 60-90 days better captures long-term behavior. The original study ran 94 days.
Step 12
Weekly Monitoring & Maintenance
  • Check for data gaps or sensor disconnections
  • Verify voltage trend is as expected (gradual decline)
  • Confirm no BMS fault alarms
  • Verify backup data exports are current
  • Visual inspection of battery area (no swelling, leaks, unusual heat)
  • Record any environmental changes (temperature swings, humidity, etc.)

Part 6: Data Analysis

Step 13
Export & Process Data
  1. Export time-series voltage data to CSV format
  2. Include columns:
    • Timestamp (ISO 8601 format recommended)
    • Voltage (mV or V)
    • Temperature (if available)
    • Any other relevant sensors
  3. Clean data:
    • Remove duplicates
    • Flag outliers (>3σ from rolling mean)
    • Document any known sensor failures or gaps
Step 14
Calculate Key Metrics

Voltage Drift Rate:

  1. Apply moving average filter (MA-60s recommended)
  2. Perform linear regression: V(t) = β₀ + β₁·t
  3. Drift rate = β₁ (mV/day)
  4. Report R² to assess fit quality

Hourly Spread:

  1. Group data by hour
  2. Calculate max - min within each hour
  3. Plot over time to detect measurement regime changes

Temperature Correlation (if available):

  1. Calculate Pearson correlation between voltage and temperature
  2. Perform linear regression to get coefficient (mV/°F or mV/°C)
Analysis scripts available in repository: analysis/drift_analysis.py and analysis/spread_analysis.py

Compare Results

Expected results for replication:

Drift rate (early) −0.5 to −1.0 mV/day _________
Drift rate (late) −0.1 to −0.5 mV/day _________

  • Review methodology for differences

  • Verify sensor calibration

  • Document findings and consider sharing in GitHub discussions

Troubleshooting Common Issues

SmartShunt Not Appearing in Bluetooth

  • Verify power is connected to shunt
  • Check battery voltage is >11V (shunt requires power to operate)
  • Move closer to device (Bluetooth range ~10m)
  • Reset shunt by disconnecting power for 30 seconds

Voltage Readings Seem Too High/Low

  • Verify voltage sense wire is connected to correct terminal
  • Check for voltage drops across fuses or connections
  • Compare to known-good multimeter reading
  • Re-zero the shunt with no current flowing

Large Data Gaps in Logs

  • Check Bluetooth connection stability (move logger closer if needed)
  • Verify logger system uptime (check for crashes/reboots)
  • Increase polling interval if connection is unreliable
  • Consider switching to wired connection (RS485, CAN) for critical applications

Drift Rate Much Higher Than Expected

  • Check for parasitic loads (BMS draws 20-50 mA typically)
  • Verify no balance function is active (some BMS balance at rest)
  • Confirm temperature environment is stable
  • Consider cell quality (Grade B/C cells have higher self-discharge)

BMS Keeps Disconnecting

  • Verify protection settings aren't too aggressive
  • Check individual cell voltages aren't at limits
  • Confirm temperature sensors (if equipped) are reading correctly
  • Ensure current draw doesn't exceed BMS rating
