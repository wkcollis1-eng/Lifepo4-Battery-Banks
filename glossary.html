📖 Glossary

Technical terms, abbreviations, and definitions used throughout the LiFePO₄ battery study

This glossary provides definitions for technical terminology, statistical concepts, and hardware-specific terms used in the battery monitoring research. Terms are organized by category for easy navigation.

Chemistry Hardware Metrics Statistical Electrical General

Battery Chemistry & Behavior

Architectural Immunity Core Concept
The principle that parallel-connected lithium cells achieve monolithic behavior through electrical topology rather than cell-level matching. Properly connected cells naturally share a common bus potential regardless of brand, age, or internal resistance variations.
In this study: Validated through 94+ days of monitoring showing no evidence of voltage divergence at the bus level despite using mixed-brand cells.
LiFePO₄ (LFP)
Lithium Iron Phosphate
A type of lithium-ion battery chemistry using iron phosphate as the cathode material. Known for superior safety, long cycle life (3000+ cycles), and stable discharge voltage compared to other lithium chemistries.
Characteristics: Nominal voltage 3.2V per cell, flat discharge curve, non-flammable chemistry, lower energy density than NMC/NCA but much safer.
SOC (State of Charge)
The current energy level of a battery expressed as a percentage of its total capacity. 100% = fully charged, 0% = fully discharged.
For LiFePO₄: ~13.6V = 100% SOC, ~13.0V = 80% SOC, ~12.8V = 50% SOC, ~10.8V = 0% SOC (for 12V bank).
Self-Discharge
The gradual loss of charge when a battery sits idle with no external load. Measured as voltage drift over time or equivalent current draw.
This study: Measured at −0.67 mV/day during stasis (equivalent to ~13-20 mA self-discharge for a 500 Ah bank).
Cell Balancing
The process of equalizing charge levels across cells in a series battery pack. BMS may perform passive balancing (bleeding excess charge as heat) or active balancing (redistributing charge).
Note: Most consumer BMS perform passive balancing only when cells exceed a threshold voltage difference (typically >30-50 mV).
Grade A vs Grade B Cells
Quality classification for lithium cells. Grade A cells meet manufacturer's capacity and internal resistance specifications. Grade B cells have higher internal resistance or reduced capacity but are still functional.
This study: Uses Grade A prismatic cells. Grade B cells may show higher divergence in parallel configurations.

Hardware & Instrumentation

BMS (Battery Management System)
Electronic system that monitors and protects battery cells from over-voltage, under-voltage, over-current, and temperature extremes. May also perform cell balancing.
This study: JBD 4S 200A BMS with Bluetooth telemetry and cell-level voltage monitoring.
Victron SmartShunt
A battery monitor that measures voltage, current, and calculates state of charge. Uses Bluetooth Low Energy for wireless data transmission. Resolution: 10 mV voltage, ±1% accuracy.
Primary sensor: Provides bus-level voltage telemetry every 10 seconds for this study.
Eco Mode
Power-saving feature in Victron devices that reduces sampling frequency when battery is at rest. Activates automatically after extended idle period to conserve power.
Impact on study: Activated on day 71, causing increased apparent voltage spread due to sparse sampling (measurement artifact, not battery divergence).
Class T Fuse
Fast-acting current-limiting fuse designed for DC applications. Essential safety device for lithium battery banks to prevent catastrophic short circuit events.
Safety requirement: Always install at battery terminals. A 500 Ah bank can deliver thousands of amps in a short circuit.
4S Configuration
4 cells in Series
Battery configuration with 4 cells connected in series to achieve 12V nominal (3.2V × 4 = 12.8V). Multiple 4S groups can be connected in parallel to increase capacity.
This study: Multiple parallel 4S groups create 12V 500Ah total capacity.
Prismatic Cells
Rectangular-shaped lithium battery cells (vs cylindrical). Easier to stack and configure in battery banks. Common in DIY and commercial energy storage systems.
Home Assistant
Open-source home automation platform. Used in this study as the data logging framework to collect SmartShunt telemetry via Bluetooth and store in InfluxDB time-series database.

Metrics & Measurements

Voltage Drift Key Metric
The rate of voltage change over time when a battery is at rest (no load). Measured in mV/day. Negative drift indicates voltage decline (self-discharge).
drift_rate = (V_end - V_start) / days
This study: −0.67 mV/day (full stasis), −0.17 mV/day (last 30 days).
Spread Important
In this study, the hourly maximum−minimum voltage measured from a single bus-level sensor. This is NOT a measure of cell-to-cell voltage difference.
spread_hourly = max(V_samples_in_hour) - min(V_samples_in_hour)
Critical distinction: Reflects measurement noise and sampling artifacts, not actual voltage divergence between cells. Per-cell sensing would be required to measure cell-to-cell variation.
MA-60s (Moving Average, 60 samples)
A smoothing filter that averages voltage over a rolling 60-sample window (10 minutes at 10-second sampling). Reduces high-frequency noise while preserving trends.
Effectiveness: Achieved 42-50% noise reduction in this study depending on segment regularity.
Temperature Coefficient
The change in battery voltage per degree of temperature change, typically expressed in mV/°F or mV/°C. Includes both cell chemistry effects and measurement chain thermal sensitivity.
coefficient = Δvoltage / Δtemperature
This study: +1.0 ± 0.3 mV/°F system-level (ambient temperature, not battery core).
Usable Capacity
The actual energy that can be extracted from a battery from full charge to the BMS cutoff voltage. Often less than rated capacity due to conservative cutoff settings or cell quality.
This study: 397 Ah measured vs 500 Ah rated = 99.3% validation (accounting for actual cell specs vs labeled capacity).
Bus-Level Measurement
Voltage measurement taken at the common connection point (bus) rather than individual cell terminals. Represents the collective behavior of all parallel cells.
Limitation: Cannot detect individual cell imbalances, only aggregate bank behavior.

Statistical & Analysis Terms

Linear Regression
Statistical method for finding the best-fit straight line through data points. Used to quantify drift rates and temperature correlations.
y = β₀ + β₁·x + ε where β₁ = slope (drift rate)
R² (R-squared / Coefficient of Determination)
Statistical measure of how well a regression line fits the data. Range: 0 to 1, where 1 = perfect fit. R² > 0.95 indicates very strong linear correlation.
σ (Sigma / Standard Deviation)
Measure of variability in a dataset. In quality control, ±2σ bounds capture ~95% of normal data. Values outside ±3σ are typically flagged as outliers.
This study: Used for noise quantification (σ_raw vs σ_MA60s) and anomaly detection via control limits.
Rolling Statistics
Calculating statistics (mean, standard deviation) over a moving time window rather than the entire dataset. Useful for tracking trends and detecting shifts in behavior.
This study: 7-day rolling windows for control limit calculation.
Pearson Correlation
Measure of linear relationship between two variables. Range: −1 to +1, where +1 = perfect positive correlation, 0 = no correlation, −1 = perfect negative correlation.
This study: Used to quantify voltage-temperature relationship over 34-day subset.
Control Limits
Statistical boundaries (typically mean ± 2σ or ± 3σ) used to detect when a process has changed or when data points are anomalous.
Application: Identifies measurement regime changes (like Eco Mode activation) and data quality issues.
Segmentation Analysis
Dividing a dataset into distinct time periods and analyzing each separately. Useful when measurement conditions or system behavior changes during the study.
This study: Separate analysis of stasis period (days 1-70) vs Eco Mode (days 71-94) vs last 30 days.

Electrical Concepts

Voltage (V)
Electrical potential difference between two points. For batteries, represents the driving force that pushes current through a circuit.
Units: Volts (V), millivolts (mV) = 0.001V. This study reports voltages in mV for precision.
Current (I)
Flow of electrical charge, measured in amperes (A). For batteries: positive current = discharge, negative current = charge.
Capacity (Ah)
Ampere-hours
Total amount of electric charge a battery can deliver. 1 Ah = 1 ampere flowing for 1 hour. A 500 Ah battery can theoretically supply 500A for 1 hour, or 1A for 500 hours.
Internal Resistance
Opposition to current flow within a battery cell. Causes voltage drop under load and heat generation. Lower is better. Measured in milliohms (mΩ).
Parallel Connection
Connecting batteries positive-to-positive and negative-to-negative. Increases total capacity while maintaining voltage. Cells naturally share current based on their internal resistance.
Series Connection
Connecting batteries positive-to-negative in a chain. Increases voltage while maintaining capacity. A 4S configuration (4 cells in series) = 4 × 3.2V = 12.8V nominal.
Bus (Busbar)
Common electrical connection point where multiple circuits meet. In battery banks, the positive and negative bus bars connect all parallel cell groups.

General Study Terms

Stasis Period
Time period when battery is at rest with no charge/discharge activity and stable sampling regime. In this study: days 1-70 with continuous 10-second sampling.
Eco Mode Period
Time period when Victron SmartShunt operated in power-save mode with intermittent sampling. In this study: days 71-94.
Baseline
Reference measurement or behavior established during controlled conditions. Used as comparison point for detecting changes or anomalies.
Aliasing
Measurement artifact that occurs when sampling rate is too low to capture actual signal variation. Can make spread appear larger than it actually is.
This study: Eco Mode's sparse sampling caused spread inflation through aliasing, not actual voltage divergence.
Time-Series Data
Data points collected at successive time intervals. Voltage measurements every 10 seconds create a time-series dataset suitable for trend analysis.
Telemetry
Automatic measurement and wireless transmission of data. SmartShunt transmits voltage readings via Bluetooth Low Energy to Home Assistant.
Open Data / Open Science
Research practice of making data, methods, and code publicly available for verification and replication. This study follows open science principles.
