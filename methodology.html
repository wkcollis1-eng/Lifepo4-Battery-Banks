1. Study Design Overview

Objective

Validate long-term storage performance and architectural immunity of a DIY mixed-brand LiFePO₄ battery bank through continuous voltage telemetry and controlled discharge testing.

Architectural Immunity: The principle that parallel-connected lithium cells achieve monolithic behavior through electrical topology rather than cell-level matching. Properly connected cells share a common bus potential regardless of brand, age, or internal resistance variations.

Study Phases

Phase Duration Purpose Key Outputs
Discharge Testing Oct 2025 Validate usable capacity 397 Ah measured (99.3% of rated)
Stasis Monitoring 70 days Measure voltage drift at rest −0.67 mV/day drift rate
Eco Mode 24 days Assess measurement regime change Spread inflation observed
Temperature Analysis 34 days subset Quantify thermal coefficient +1.0 mV/°F system-level

Study Period

October 29, 2025 – January 31, 2026 (94+ days continuous monitoring)

2. Hardware Configuration

Battery Bank Specifications

Component Specification Notes
System Voltage 12V nominal 13.2-13.6V typical operating range
Total Capacity 500 Ah rated 397 Ah validated via discharge test
Cell Chemistry LiFePO₄ (LFP) Prismatic cells, Grade A
Configuration 4S parallel Mixed brands in parallel topology
BMS JBD 4S 200A Bluetooth-enabled, cell-level monitoring
Primary Sensor Victron SmartShunt 500A Bus-level voltage telemetry

Mixed-Brand Configuration

The battery bank intentionally uses multiple cell brands to test architectural immunity. All cells are Grade A prismatic LiFePO₄ with similar capacity ratings but from different manufacturers.

Safety Note: All configurations include Class T fuses at battery terminals and BMS protection for over-voltage, under-voltage, over-current, and temperature limits.

3. Data Collection Protocol

Measurement System

  • Primary Instrument: Victron SmartShunt 500A
    • Voltage resolution: 10 mV (0.01V)
    • Accuracy: ±1% on voltage measurements
    • Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) telemetry
  • Data Logging: Home Assistant with InfluxDB backend
    • Sampling interval: 10 seconds (native SmartShunt)
    • Storage: Time-series database with no aggregation
    • Backup: Daily exports to CSV
  • Temperature Monitoring: Ambient temperature via Home Assistant weather integration
    • Co-located with battery bank
    • Used for correlation analysis only

Sampling Regime Changes

Two distinct measurement periods occurred due to Victron Eco Mode activation:

Period Mode Sampling Behavior Impact
Days 1-70 Normal Continuous 10s intervals Baseline noise floor
Days 71-94 Eco Mode Intermittent (power-save) Increased apparent spread
Critical Distinction: "Spread" in this study refers to hourly max−min voltage from a single bus-level sensor, not cell-to-cell voltage differences. Eco Mode's sparse sampling inflates observed spread through aliasing, not due to actual divergence.

Data Quality Control

  • Outlier Detection: Values >3σ from rolling mean flagged for review
  • Gap Analysis: No significant data gaps observed (>99.9% uptime)
  • Sensor Validation: Cross-checked against BMS voltage readings weekly
  • Calibration: SmartShunt zeroed pre-study, no recalibration during monitoring

4. Key Metrics & Definitions

Voltage Drift

drift_rate = (V_end - V_start) / days

Measured in mV/day. Negative values indicate voltage decline over time.

Spread

spread_hourly = max(V_samples_in_hour) - min(V_samples_in_hour)

Important: This is not a measure of cell-to-cell voltage difference. It represents measurement variability at a single bus point.

Moving Average (MA-60s)

MA-60s = rolling_mean(voltage, window=60_samples)

60-sample (10-minute) moving average applied to reduce high-frequency noise. Achieved 42-50% noise reduction depending on segment.

Temperature Coefficient

coefficient = Δvoltage / Δtemperature (mV/°F)

System-level thermal sensitivity. Includes both pack chemistry and measurement chain effects.

Capacity Validation

Controlled discharge test:

  • Discharge at constant current to BMS cutoff (10.8V)
  • Integration: capacity = ∫ current × time
  • Result: 397 Ah from 500 Ah rated (99.3% validation considering actual cell specs)

Projected Storage Endurance

days_to_80%_SOC = (V_current - V_80%) / drift_rate

Where V_80% = ~13.0V for LiFePO₄. Calculated using late-period drift rate (−0.17 mV/day) for conservative estimate.

5. Statistical Methods

Linear Regression (Drift Analysis)

Applied to time-series voltage data to quantify drift rates:

V(t) = β₀ + β₁·t + ε
  • β₁ = slope (drift rate in mV/day)
  • R² reported to assess fit quality
  • Calculated for multiple time windows: full study, early stasis, late period

Rolling Statistics

Control limits established using rolling windows:

  • Window size: 7-day rolling mean and standard deviation
  • Control limits: Mean ± 2σ for anomaly detection
  • Application: Identifies measurement regime changes and outliers

Correlation Analysis

Pearson correlation between voltage and temperature:

r = cov(V, T) / (σ_V · σ_T)

Applied to 34-day temperature subset. Coefficient calculated via linear regression slope.

Noise Reduction Assessment

Quantified effectiveness of MA-60s filtering:

noise_reduction = 1 - (σ_MA-60s / σ_raw)

Result: 42-50% reduction depending on segment.

Segmentation Analysis

Data analyzed in distinct phases to isolate effects:

  1. Full stasis period (days 1-70): Baseline drift characterization
  2. Eco Mode period (days 71-94): Measurement regime impact
  3. Last 30 days: Late-stage drift assessment
  4. Temperature subset (34 days): Thermal correlation

6. Validity & Limitations

Internal Validity Strengths

  • ✅ 94+ days continuous monitoring (no significant gaps)
  • ✅ Controlled discharge test validates capacity claims
  • ✅ Temperature correlation analysis on 34-day subset
  • ✅ Multiple time-window drift analyses show consistency
  • ✅ Documented measurement regime change (Eco Mode) with clear impact

Known Limitations

  • ⚠️ Single sensor: Bus-level voltage only, no per-cell monitoring
  • ⚠️ Temperature data: Ambient only (not battery core temperature)
  • ⚠️ Limited load cycles: Stasis study, not cycling performance
  • ⚠️ Sample size: N=1 battery bank configuration
  • ⚠️ Brand specifics: Mixed-brand config not fully documented (proprietary cell sources)

Measurement Uncertainties

Parameter Uncertainty Source
Voltage ±10 mV SmartShunt resolution + sensor accuracy
Drift Rate ±0.1 mV/day Regression fit + sampling noise
Temperature ±2°F Ambient sensor, not battery core
Capacity ±5 Ah Current integration + cutoff timing

Threats to External Validity

  • Climate specificity: Connecticut climate (Zone 5A), indoor storage
  • Single configuration: Results may not generalize to all mixed-brand setups
  • Operating point: ~80-85% SOC range, not full charge cycle

7. Replication Criteria

Minimum Equipment Requirements

  • LiFePO₄ battery bank (any capacity ≥100 Ah recommended)
  • Voltage monitoring with ≤10 mV resolution
  • Data logging at ≤1 minute intervals
  • Minimum 30-day monitoring duration
  • Temperature recording (ambient acceptable)

Expected Replication Results

Metric Expected Range Validation
Capacity >95% of rated Via controlled discharge
Drift rate −0.5 to −1.0 mV/day Early stasis period
Hourly spread <50 mV typical Normal sampling mode
MA-60s reduction 40-55% Depends on sampling regularity

Divergence from Expected Results

If replication produces significantly different results, investigate:

  • Cell quality (Grade B vs Grade A cells may show higher divergence)
  • Connection resistance (poor bus connections cause voltage drops)
  • Temperature extremes (>40°F swings may amplify drift)
  • BMS interference (some BMS perform cell balancing even at rest)
  • Measurement aliasing (sampling intervals >1 min may increase spread)
Open Science: Complete datasets and analysis code are available in the repository's /data and /analysis directories for independent validation.
